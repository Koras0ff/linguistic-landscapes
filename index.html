About this interface

LILABI — Linguistic Landscape of Bishkek

LILABI (Linguistic Landscape of Bishkek) provides the largest open-access dataset of linguistic landscape for Bishkek, the capital city of Kyrgyzstan. The data were collected between October 19 and 27, 2024, during a field trip to Bishkek organized by HALS (the Helsinki Diversity Linguistics Group). For ethical reasons, the people and plates in the photographs were manually cleaned up.

What it is

An interactive map of photographed public signs collected in Bishkek. Every sign is geolocated and hand-coded for its concept category and subcategory, linguality, directedness, discourse type, language(s), and script(s). One photo can hold several signs, and each sign is its own point.

What it has

  • Filter layers for every coded attribute, with ANY/ALL matching and search.
  • Colour-by any attribute, adjustable marker size, and fit-to-selection.
  • A linguistic-diversity heatmap (Simpson index) over an adjustable map grid.
  • The four Bishkek districts drawn as a highlight layer, with live sign counts.
  • A selection-analysis panel: composition bars, a language/attribute co-occurrence matrix, and monolingual-vs-multilingual summary stats.
  • Click any sign for its photo and full metadata.

What researchers can do

  • Filter to any subset and read its composition and diversity update live.
  • See where linguistic diversity concentrates spatially, and compare districts.
  • Ask which languages or scripts co-occur on the same signs.
  • Inspect individual signs to ground the numbers in the actual photographs.

Full-size images & full corpus

The photographs shown here are downscaled for the web. The full-size images and the complete corpus are archived at Kielipankki (the Language Bank of Finland): kielipankki.fi/corpora/lilabi.
